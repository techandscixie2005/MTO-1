## Metric and selection rules

The primary metric is pooled raw oscillator strength (`f`) R² on the frozen validation split: 6,686 molecules × 10 states, with every valid label retained. Checkpoint selection remains each study’s preregistered rule, usually lowest pooled validation raw-f SSE; validation-selected values are conditional on repeated use of this split and are not fresh holdout estimates. No report in this synthesis removes molecule 14562 or any other case.

For historical tail analyses, q90=.0546 and q99=.2377 are **validation-derived** f cutoffs. The prospective final-test protocol uses training-only raw-f cutoffs q90=.0549 and q99=.2412. Geometry q2 bins are TRAIN-derived. See the additive [tail-threshold provenance erratum](TAIL_THRESHOLD_PROVENANCE_ERRATUM.md) and [provenance record](TAIL_THRESHOLD_PROVENANCE_ERRATUM.json). Do not describe the historical validation thresholds as TRAIN-derived.

## Current candidate and limits

F5 remains the top accepted validation recipe: the equal-weight mean of eta0, eta01, eta1, G1, and G3. Root has frozen its component checkpoints, weights, sources, preprocessing, and evaluator scope in [CANDIDATE_FREEZE.json](final_f5_historical_test/CANDIDATE_FREEZE.json), with the [pretest protocol](final_f5_historical_test/PROTOCOL.md) and [provenance](final_f5_historical_test/PROVENANCE.json). This is preparation only. The final historical-test comparison remains **PENDING** until the evaluator implementation, preflight and independent review are sealed, the reviewed package is published from its D: archive, and root separately authorizes execution. The protocol fixes F5 versus the previous equal-three as the primary comparison and eta0 as context; it permits no recipe changes after test. The historical test has been reused, so any authorized comparison is exploratory, not untouched-holdout confirmation. No test access or further candidate tuning is authorized by this draft.

The results favor a modest conclusion: equal-weight model diversity improved pooled validation performance, but added seeds, scratch readouts, descriptor features, and the tested frozen corrections did not beat F5. Repeated validation selection, one or few seeds, historical test reuse, and unresolved bright-tail/state-specific errors limit generalization claims. F5’s strongest evidence is validation-only; its test performance is unknown.
